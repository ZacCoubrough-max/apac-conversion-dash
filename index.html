Market ranking rules and signal levels

Each market calculates its own traffic and selling-product benchmarks from the uploaded data. The current-view floor is the higher of 30 views and the 75th percentile of positive current views. The high-traffic floor is the higher of 100 views and the 90th percentile. Own-history comparisons require views in two prior periods and average weekly views of at least half the current-view floor, with a minimum of 10.

Conversion gaps ranks products below their own historical conversion, with at least one item expected at that rate. Improving products needs two purchases, higher conversion and at least one item of gain at current views. Zero-purchase lists require high views and exactly zero purchases; prior sales are not required.

Consistent performers needs two current purchases, conversion at or above the historical selling-product median and at least two prior periods meeting that conversion benchmark with views above the history floor and at least one purchase. Exposure opportunities needs two purchases, conversion at or above the historical selling-product upper quartile, and either below-median selling-product views or views at least 25% below own history while retaining at least 90% of own historical conversion.

Established selling products have at least four purchases across the prior periods and enough average weekly views. If fewer than ten peers qualify, the benchmark uses products with at least one historical purchase. Peer comparisons remain within the same market; no brand, price or category matching is implied.

Stronger signal: conversion gaps needs five expected items, an item gap of three, and a decline of at least 20% of own baseline and 10% of the market’s combined historical conversion (minimum 0.01 percentage points). Improving needs five purchases, a gain of three items and the equivalent positive conversion change. Zero purchases needs usable history and three expected items. Consistent performers needs five current purchases and three qualifying prior periods with two purchases each. Exposure opportunities needs five purchases. The last two also need ten benchmark peers. Other eligible candidates are labelled Worth monitoring.

Lists rank stronger signals first, then item gap or gain, views for zero purchases, consistency for performers, or purchased items for exposure. These are review heuristics, not statistical significance tests. Counts are item quantities, not matched shopper journeys. Unusual aggregate ratios stay in All products for review. Item gaps and gains hold current views constant; they are scenarios, not forecasts. A product may appear in several categories. The app shows fewer than ten when genuine candidates are unavailable.

WEEKLY PRODUCT REVIEW

Your files. Your market. Clear priorities.

For trading, merchandising, marketing and digital operations teams reviewing product demand and conversion.

1 · Upload and check

Choose a market from Markets, then upload its Excel .xlsx workbook. L7 Days through L35 Days map automatically. GA4 metadata and totals above the actual column headers are skipped automatically.

The app reads Last Run On from each report tab and sets L7 to the seven days ending the day before that run. This keeps older exports correctly labelled. All five reports must have run on the same date. If no readable run date is stored, enter the actual L7 end date in the fallback field.

Reports with a known row-count shortfall, missing required columns or blank breakdown are blocked. Files without GA4 completeness metadata are marked unverified; reconcile them with GA4 before making decisions. Only rows in the files can be analysed.

2 · Compare correctly

WOW / vs LW compares the selected period with its immediate predecessor. For the current period, the historical comparison uses all four uploaded prior periods (L14, L21, L28 and L35). Older periods have shorter history and are labelled accordingly.

Weekly volume baseline = sum of L14, L21, L28 and L35 totals ÷ 4. ATC and conversion baseline = combined lookback numerator ÷ combined views, not an average of weekly percentages. A missing product row contributes zero in that period; missing files are never treated as zero.

L7 is compared with L14 for WOW and with the weekly average of L14, L21, L28 and L35 for the four-week comparison. Each tab is a separate seven-day period.

Keep the same GA4 property, filters, timezone and reporting scope across the five reports. Product conversion = items purchased ÷ items viewed × 100. ATC rate = items added to cart ÷ items viewed × 100.

3 · Key products and full results

Key products shows an independent ranked shortlist of up to ten candidates per category, using benchmarks calculated within the selected market. Select a category card to open its shortlist on a separate screen; a product can appear in more than one list. Stronger signal and Worth monitoring labels distinguish evidence levels; when fewer than ten meet the basic ranking criteria, only available candidates are shown. Select a product to inspect its five-period trend, volumes, ATC rate and purchase/cart ratio. Use View all matching products to open the full filtered list.

All products includes every product in the selected period, aggregated by Item ID. Use search, highlight, review-signal and view-volume filters. Choose 25, 50 or 100 rows per page; sorting applies to all matching products. Export filtered CSV includes all matching pages, in the displayed sort order.

The baseline combines prior purchases and views before calculating conversion. Item gaps and gains measure a scenario at current views, not proven lost sales. Traffic floors, selling-product benchmarks and signal-level rules are listed under Market ranking rules and signal levels. Groups overlap. Products with low volume or limited history stay in the full list.

4 · Review and share

Open a product to review its weekly history and conversion trend. Use Export filtered CSV in All products to share the matching products.

Optional Veracity workbooks and catalogue CSVs match GA4 Item ID to shopify_id. The Brand Ref is shown where available, and the exported product handle or URL opens the product on the selected storefront.

Uploads stay in memory in this tab. Closing or refreshing removes imported performance data. Performance files are processed locally. External links open only when clicked. The demo is sample data.
