How to interpret product health, evidence and opportunity

Review priorities are relative to the selected market, not proof of a product or checkout fault. Compare like-for-like products manually when price, launch timing or promotion differs.

Only four weeks are loaded. The latest week uses the preceding three available weeks; older weeks use fewer. With no prior history, the selected week provides a provisional market comparison. Status movement uses each week's available baseline and can change as that baseline changes.

Evidence: Limited below the traffic floor or fewer than three observed/expected purchases; Moderate from three; Stronger from ten. Expected purchases = views × market median conversion. Fewer than ten eligible baseline products or unusual funnel counts cap evidence at Limited; same-week baselines cap it at Moderate. These are practical review rules, not statistical significance tests.

Repeated weakness means two or more consecutive numbered weeks below the selected week's market review thresholds. It stops when a week or product is missing, has too little traffic, or is no longer weak. Earlier weeks are rechecked against these same thresholds.

Zero quartile cutoffs use half the market median instead. Weak ATC, conversion and cart-ratio flags require a positive market median and at least three expected ATCs/purchases for the relevant ratio. Winners need positive upper-quartile conversion and at least three purchases (or the higher market purchase floor).

Estimated revenue opportunity = max(0, views × market median conversion − purchased items) × the product's observed average selling price. Price uses selected and baseline weeks and requires at least three purchased items and positive revenue; otherwise revenue opportunity is unavailable. The item gap remains visible. This scenario assumes the product could reach market median conversion; it is not a sales forecast.

Views, ATCs and purchases are aggregate item counts, not matched customers. Funnel ratios can exceed 100%. Validate tracking, stock/sizes, price, launch timing, promotions, delivery and fulfilment before assigning an action. No category or price-peer benchmark is inferred from these columns.

Priority lists put repeated weakness first, then evidence and estimated item gap. Growth lists favour evidence and purchases/conversion. Revenue estimates do not determine the shortlist on their own.

YOUR WEEKLY PRODUCT REVIEW

From product signals to clear actions.

A practical guide to what this report does, who should use it and how to make decisions from it.

What is this dashboard?

A weekly product performance report for Australia, New Zealand, Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand. It connects item views, add-to-carts, purchases and revenue to highlight products to grow, improve or investigate.

Performance is assessed within each individual market. The report uses WOW comparisons and the latest four available data weeks. It does not include LFL, brand or category analysis.

Who is it for?

  • Ecommerce and trading teams: identify product priorities and own the weekly review.
  • Merchandising and buying: investigate availability, price and assortment; agree where exposure can grow.
  • Marketing: find candidates for additional visibility and check whether demand converts.
  • Digital operations and analytics: validate tracking and investigate product-page, delivery or fulfilment signals.

How to run your weekly review

  1. Confirm the data source. Check the pill at the top. “Live · Cloudflare” is the Worker feed; “Apps Script fallback” is the legacy feed. “Demo” uses sample products and must not be used for trading decisions. Read any feed warning before reviewing. Cloudflare loads small pages and shows progress; performance results appear only after all pages have loaded.
  2. Select a market and reporting week. Start with the latest complete week in the sheet. The newest available week may still be incomplete. Review the Current, Previous and Health baseline labels. With four weeks loaded, the latest week has up to three prior weeks for its baseline.
  3. Read the topline funnel. Review item views, ATC rate, conversion and revenue with their WOW changes. Click a KPI tile to see weekly results. Check whether weakness is broad across the market or concentrated in products.
  4. Open a Product Health status. Each tile opens up to 50 priority products. Click Meaning for the definition and current market rules. Counts are capped at 50; the tile also indicates the total where there are more products.
  5. Validate each priority. Use Open product ↗ to open a matched product in a new tab. Links match the selected market and exact Shopify Item ID; unmatched products remain visible. Check evidence strength, repeated weakness, volume, the reason and the estimated item gap. Search by Item ID or name, filter view volume and sort columns. Search also shows up to 50 results; low-data products are excluded.
  6. Agree a concrete action. Check stock and size coverage, price, promotion, launch timing, page content, delivery and tracking using your other tools. Use Add beside a product to record the action, owner, due date and notes.
  7. Review and follow up. Update action status next week. Export the Action Tracker CSV to share outcomes. Actions are saved in this browser and are not automatically shared with colleagues or synced across devices.

Rules have a priority order, so a product receives one status even if several signals apply. Products below the market traffic floor are excluded from status lists. A status is a review priority, not proof of a fault.

Understand the measures

  • ATC rate: items added to cart ÷ items viewed.
  • Product conversion: items purchased ÷ items viewed.
  • Cart ratio: items purchased ÷ items added to cart.
  • WOW: volume/revenue changes are percentages; changes in rates are percentage points (pp).

These are aggregate item counts, not unique people or matched customer journeys. Ratios can exceed 100%. The previous period is the preceding available week; check the displayed week numbers if a sheet week is missing.

Evidence and repeated weakness

Limited, Moderate and Stronger describe practical evidence strength using views, purchases and expected purchases at market median conversion. Hover over an evidence label for its detail. They do not express statistical certainty.

“2+ weeks weak” identifies consecutive numbered weeks below the selected week's market thresholds. Missing or low-volume weeks break the streak. Status movement uses each week's available baseline, so it can also reflect benchmark changes.

Open “How to interpret product health, evidence and opportunity” on the Dashboard for the exact rules and current thresholds.

Estimated opportunity

The estimated item gap is the extra purchased items needed to reach the market median conversion at the product's current view volume.

Estimated revenue opportunity multiplies that gap by the product's observed average selling price across the selected and baseline weeks. It requires at least three purchased items and positive revenue. A dash means no usable price estimate; the item gap is still visible.

This is a scenario for prioritising investigation, not a forecast or guaranteed lost sales. Different prices and product types may justify different conversion levels.

Use the report with context

The sheet does not include stock, size coverage, categories, customer sessions or product-level campaign attribution. Confirm these elsewhere before deciding why a product is weak.

Historical context is limited: older selectable weeks have fewer prior weeks, and the earliest uses a provisional same-week market comparison. Week numbers are treated as one fiscal sequence; the report has no year selector.

If data fails, use the source warning and investigate the API before making decisions. Refresh retries the feed; Demo data lets you explore the report's controls.
